# Carlin-type gold: descriptive model and proxy options

Condensed from the project's "Descriptive Model of Carlin-Type Gold Deposits" (after Hofstra 1999; Hofstra and Cline 2000; Cline et al. 2005; Radtke 1985; Bagby and Berger 1985). Use it as the default when the user has no descriptive model of their own, and ask where the user's area differs.

## The model in brief
- Epigenetic, stratabound replacement gold in Paleozoic calcareous sedimentary rocks. Gold is submicron ("invisible") in arsenian pyrite and marcasite.
- Nevada age 42-36 Ma (Eocene), after removal of the Farallon slab: extension, high heat flow, calc-alkaline magmatism.
- Hosts: thin-bedded, pyritic, carbonaceous, silty dolomitic limestone and marl of the miogeoclinal shelf-slope transition. Reactive because of ferroan carbonate, iron-bearing clays and diagenetic pyrite.
- Caprock: a regional impermeable siliciclastic allochthon (Roberts Mountains allochthon); deposits concentrate in the lower plate, often in windows.
- Structure: deep NNW- and WNW-striking basement faults (reactivated rift structures) as conduits; fault intersections favour large orebodies; anticlines and domes are traps; most ore lies in fault footwalls and beneath aquitards.
- Rheological contrasts: pre-ore Jurassic-Cretaceous plutons and hornfels aureoles focus fracturing.
- Alteration (subtle): sulfidation, decarbonatization (collapse breccia, jasperoid), argillization, silicification, then carbonate and dolomite halos outward. Gold grade correlates poorly with alteration intensity.
- Geochemistry: Au + As + Sb + Hg + Tl with negligible Cu, Pb, Zn and Ag; Au/Ag above 10; As, Sb, Hg anomalies are broader than Au in stream sediment and soil. Introduced: S, Au, As, Sb, Hg, Tl, Ag, Ba (+/-Te, W, Se, Mo, Cu). Removed: carbonate C, Ca, Mg, Sr, Mn, Na. Immobile: Al, Ti, Th, Zr.
- Geophysics: regional gravity gradients mark crustal discontinuities; aeromagnetics show buried intrusions and basement fabric; IP shows pyrite halos; resistivity lows mark argillized and decarbonatized zones; ASTER/Landsat map clays and iron oxides; DEM/SAR lineaments show structural corridors.
- Fluids: 150-250 C (mostly 180-240 C), low salinity, CO2-bearing, H2S-rich, pH 4-5.5. Gold precipitates by sulfidation of reactive iron.
- Depth: economic portions formed at about 0.5-3 km. Oxide ore extends to about 0-400 m. Supergene alunite (30-8 Ma) records exhumation.
- Global analogues named in the model: West Qinling and Dian-Qian-Gui (China), Zarshuran and Agdarreh (Iran), Mesel (Indonesia). Genetic debate: amagmatic (Ilchik and Barton 1997) and epizonal-intrusion (Henry and Boden 1998) models remain viable for some districts.

## Mineral-system parts and the evidence layers
| Part | Idea | Layer(s) | Data needed | Alternatives to ask about |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Source | Magmatic and basement metal source | Intrusion proximity in the age window; older intrusions; basement distance; magmatic fertility; crustal gradient | Dated igneous rocks, geologic map, geochemistry (Sr, Y, La), gravity | Local intrusion ages replace 42-36 Ma; drop intrusion layers if the model is amagmatic |
| Energy | Heat engine | Intrusive belt density; volcanic and intrusive proximity; buried plutons; mantle upwelling | Dated igneous rocks, geologic map, gravity and magnetics | Geothermal or heat-flow data if available |
| Pathway | Plumbing | Fault density; fault intersections; fault network connectivity; structural complexity; selected lithologic contacts | Faults with class (conduit or thrust); geologic map | Lineaments from DEM or SAR where faults are poorly mapped |
| Trap | Reactive host, seal, footprint | Reactive host; impermeable cap; alteration footprint; ore-fluid footprint | Geologic map; radiometrics or satellite alteration; geochemistry | Alteration proxy: potassium depletion (radiometrics), carbonate loss or silicification (map and geochemistry), clay mapping (ASTER). Fluid proxy: Au/Ag with base-metal absence, or As-Sb-Tl-Hg over immobile elements |
| Preservation | Survival and depth | Depth classes under cover | Depth to basement or cover thickness, cover mask | Erosion-level indicators if cover is thin |

## Questions to put to the user about their area
1. Which rocks are the reactive host and which seal? Are the units named differently from Nevada's?
2. What is the mineralization age, and which intrusions or volcanics are of that age or older?
3. Which faults are conduits and which are thrusts or aquitards?
4. Which alteration style is documented, and which dataset would show it?
5. Which pathfinder elements are anomalous in the geochemistry (As, Sb, Hg, Tl, others)? Is gold assayed?
6. Is the target exposed or under cover? How deep is basement?
